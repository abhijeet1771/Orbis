## 1. What testers say: how regression scope is decided after a change, and what goes wrong

### Takeaway
In practice, regression scope is decided by experience and judgment (the tester's memory of what broke before, what the developer says changed, and a fixed "core" list). Systematic selection is rare, and when tools select automatically, teams keep a full-run fallback because they do not trust the skips. Three failures recur: the developer's description misses ripple effects, hidden dependencies (shared components, config, library bumps) escape the diff, and nobody writes down what was not tested and why.

### Cited Findings
**Practice: judgment, not technique**
- Industry focus group (15 participants) plus questionnaire: "regression testing needs and practices vary greatly between and within organizations and at different stages of a project", and companies "typically relied on the developers expertise and judgment rather than a systematic selection approach" (Engström & Runeson, "A qualitative survey of regression testing practices", PROFES 2010; older than 2023 but still the standard reference) — [Springer](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/a-qualitative-survey-of-regression-testing-practices/3311456); [Lund PDF](https://lup.lub.lu.se/search/files/1496661/3738209.pdf) [search summary]
- Field study, 14 developers, 450 test sessions over three months: "Almost all developers in our study performed manual test selection, and they did so in mostly ad-hoc ways." Another company field study: developers select tests "ruthlessly" instead of running the whole suite, "although they are not accurate in their selection" (Gligoric et al., ISSTA 2014 line of work) — [unpaywall 10.1145/2642937.2643019](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F2642937.2643019) [search summary]
- Goals gap: 44 papers reviewed, 56 practitioners surveyed (11 interviews, 45 questionnaires). Literature stresses fault-detection rate, while practitioners prioritise "test suite maintenance and awareness of changes" (Minhas, Petersen et al., J. Softw. Evol. Process) — [zendy 10.1002/smr.2506](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/smr.2506) [search summary]
- Adoption gap: a review of regression-testing research plus a survey of 23 practitioners estimated that "less than 4% of the published works on RT could be of interest to industry" — [MDU PDF](https://www.es.mdu.se/pdf_publications/7115.pdf) [search summary]

**The heuristics testers actually use**
- Michael Bolton (DevelopSense) frames regression as risk, not repetition: "Regression testing is focused on the risk that something that used to work in some way no longer works that way". He cites Karen Johnson's RCRCRC heuristic: "Recent (what has changed lately?) … Core (what's really common and critical?) … Risk (where is there danger, complexity, brittleness?)". He also asks "Is Regression The Biggest Risk? … Is regression really a symptom of bigger problems?" (STAREast 2013 and KWSQA 2012 slides; old but canonical) — [STAREast 2013 PDF](https://www.developsense.com/presentations/2013-05-STAREast-Regression.pdf); [KWSQA 2012 PDF](https://developsense.com/presentations/2012-09-KWSQA-Regression.pdf) [search summary]
- Ministry of Testing article "How to do good regression testing": "A good regression testing strategy encourages targeting your testing towards areas of the product that are more at risk of changing than others". It flags configuration-sensitive code and bug fixes as where new issues appear — [MoT](https://www.ministryoftesting.com/articles/how-to-do-good-regression-testing) [search summary; date not shown in summary]
- A practitioner checklist says to start from more than the release notes ("merged tickets, dependency updates, feature flags, migration scripts, and production incidents since the last release") and asks "Was anything changed that isn't in a ticket?", because developers fix small things along the way — [greelogix 2026 checklist](https://greelogix.com/blog/regression-testing-services-release-checklist-2026/); [nextpageit](https://nextpageit.com/blog/regression-testing-checklist) [search summary, vendor or agency blogs, 2025-2026]
- A risk-scoring guide: "a payment library upgrade touches everything that takes payment, even if 'checkout code' did not change" — [crosscheck.cloud](https://crosscheck.cloud/blogs/risk-based-testing-guide/) [search summary, 2025-26]
- Recording the skipped areas: logging what was skipped and why "is the step that makes risk-based testing defensible instead of just fast" — [qasimplifiedbyakash substack](https://qasimplifiedbyakash.substack.com/p/risk-based-testing-how-good-qa-engineers) [search summary]. The same advice in an older TechTarget piece: write "a list at the end of testing of what wasn't tested" — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchsoftwarequality/tip/A-software-experts-heuristic-for-regression-testing) [search summary]
- An Optimizely engineer, quoted in Built In, describes keeping a list of behaviours "they know they cannot break, like blocking users from logging in", and says that if you don't understand what will break when you introduce new code, "you'll never build enough tests" — [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/software-engineering-perspectives-regression-testing) [search summary]
- A QA lead's blog: "QA-focused release notes bridge the gap between developer changelogs and actionable testing directives" — [Yuri Kan](https://yrkan.com/blog/release-notes-qa/) [search summary, 2025-26]
- An open forum question asks whether teams rely on automated impact analysis or "it's still a manual call by the QA lead". The thread had no replies — [Hashnode](https://hashnode.com/forums/thread/how-are-you-managing-regression-bloat-in-agile) [search summary]

**What goes wrong with automated selection**
- Datadog's own documentation lists "known situations that can cause Test Impact Analysis to skip tests that should have been run", for example library dependency changes. It recommends excluding the default branch so all tests run before production, and offers an `ITR:NoSkip` commit-message override — [Datadog TIA troubleshooting](https://docs.datadoghq.com/tests/test_impact_analysis/troubleshooting/) [search summary; live docs, 2025-26]
- A practitioner post: "My test selection tool skipped 73% of the suite. That number was the bug". It describes a coverage-map bug that produced false negatives. One developer's anecdote — [dev.to](https://dev.to/breadmsa/my-test-selection-tool-skipped-73-of-the-suite-that-number-was-the-bug-49m2) [search summary]
- Meta's predictive test selection catches "over 95% of individual test failures and over 99.9% of faulty code changes", so about 5% of failing tests are knowingly skipped. Flakiness is "a key hindrance" (Machalica et al., arXiv 1810.05286, 2018/2019). Vendors recommend "an infrequent defensive full test run" — [arXiv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1810.05286); [Launchable](https://launchableinc.com/predictive-test-selection) [search summary]. Gradle's 2022 talk lists "planning for handling missed failures" as a step before adopting PTS — [DPE 2022 slides](https://dpe.org/files/sessions/2022/Foundations_of_Predictive_Test_Slides.pdf) [search summary]
- Rule-of-thumb guidance on TIA maps: "a map missing edges = missed tests". Build- and CI-config changes are hard to map, so tools treat them as "run everything" triggers — [tessl regression-suite-selector](https://tessl.io/registry/testland/regression-suite-selector) [search summary]

### Inferences
- The "competitor" Teslon must beat is not another tool. It is the tester's own judgment, plus a conversation with the developer. A report that does not beat a five-minute chat with the developer will not be read twice. The things practitioners say they miss, and that Teslon can show, are hidden dependencies (shared widget on every screen, external callers, config repo) and a written record of what was skipped and why.
- Every automated-selection product teaches its users to keep a full-run safety net. Teslon's "never a wrong skip" promise is therefore the differentiator. It will be tested by the first escaped defect in a skipped area, and the report should make that event traceable (which run, which skip, which rule).

MoT and SQA SE searches returned glossary or academic pages). The report writer should treat "what testers say" as supported by studies and published practitioners, not by fresh forum sentiment.
- Test Guild podcast episodes on regression scope were not searched (budget).

## 3. Trust: why testers accept or reject automated recommendations

### Takeaway
Trust follows calibration, not explanation volume. Users over-rely on automation that looks authoritative (automation bias) and abandon it after a visible miss (disuse). Explanations help only when they are specific and checkable. "Placebic" explanations raise trust as much as real ones, which is a risk, not a feature. For test selection, the industry norm is skips plus a safety net (full runs on main, a no-skip override). A tool that claims "never a wrong skip" must expose its uncertainty plainly, or it inherits the distrust of TIA tools.

### Cited Findings
- Automation bias is defined as over-reliance on automated recommendations, and trust calibration (matching trust to what the system can actually do) is the fix. Review in AI & Society, 2025 — [AI & Society 2025 PDF](https://www.iris.unict.it/retrieve/d096f7ef-6ba2-4684-a598-85fb55228c10/2025_AI%26amp%3bSociety.pdf) [search summary]
- Explanations can backfire. A 2026 decision-support study found explainable AI "may also increase negative consultations" — [Decision Support Systems 2026 PDF, Paderborn](https://wiwi.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin-wiwi/dep3ls7/1-s2.0-S0167923626001107-main.pdf). A Berkeley report: explanations "do not always aid trust calibration, and can actually hurt it, especially in the face of novice users who have low self-competence" — [Berkeley capstone](https://ischool.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/sproject_attachments/humanai_capstonereport-final.pdf). Effects of explanations on trust "cannot be generalized between different decision workflows" — [arXiv 2412.15948](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2412.15948) [all search summary]
- Placebic explanations: Eiband et al., CHI 2019, found "placebic explanations invoked perceived levels of trust similar to real explanations" (seen only through a secondary page; verify against the paper) [search summary]
- In developer tools, Markus Borg argues that "user interaction that conveys an appropriate level of trust is a key safeguard" for AI refactoring in IDEs — [arXiv 2603.05229](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05229v1) [search summary, 2026]
- Test-selection products build in distrust. Datadog runs everything on the default branch and offers an `ITR:NoSkip` override — [Datadog docs](https://docs.datadoghq.com/tests/test_impact_analysis/troubleshooting/). Meta-style PTS needs "sampled full runs" to see what the model skipped — [BrowserStack guide](https://www.browserstack.com/guide/predictive-test-selection) [search summary]
- Developer trust in AI accuracy is falling: 46% distrust in 2025 vs 31% in 2024 (Stack Overflow) — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-developer-trust-in-ai-declines/) [search summary]
- WQR 2025-26 is framed around "the confidence gap", and Sogeti's commentary says humans "are the backbone of trust, ethics, and accountability" — [OpenText WQR](https://www.opentext.com/resources/world-quality-report); [Sogeti](https://www.sogeti.com/featured-articles/the-future-of-quality-engineering-trust-velocity-and-ai-readiness/) [search summary]
- A tester-side norm: skipped areas should be logged with the reason, so the decision "is defensible instead of just fast" — [substack](https://qasimplifiedbyakash.substack.com/p/risk-based-testing-how-good-qa-engineers) [search summary]

### Inferences (what evidence format earns trust, applied to Teslon)
1. **Checkable specifics beat volume.** One clickable file:line that a tester can open and agree with does more than "71/71 lines re-checked". Teslon has the links; the comment should show one per skip.
2. **Uncertainty shown in the verdict, not in the footer.** A "High confidence" headline above caveats (G2) is the pattern that produces disuse after the first surprise.
3. **No overclaim anywhere.** One "(Proven)" on a hedged outcome (G5) is enough for a sceptical lead to discount every Proven.
4. **A safety-net statement.** Testers are used to "skips + full run on main". Teslon should say what it recommends as the net: "skips apply to this PR's manual regression; keep your nightly full run". This reduces fear of the word "skip".
5. **Honest track record.** The report should carry the tool's measured results on this repo over time (skips later contradicted: 0 of N), not only the bench on its own fixtures (REPORT-CHECKLIST E5 ◐).
6. **"Deterministic, no AI" said once in the comment.** It answers the rising distrust measured above.

### Gaps
- Found no study specifically on testers' trust in test-impact or "safe to skip" recommendations, and no published account of a TIA tool's skip causing a production escape with a post-mortem. Teslon should consider collecting this evidence itself (pilot interviews).

## 4. Standards and vocabulary to get right

### Takeaway
Teslon's words largely fit ISTQB, but it blurs three distinctions that leads and managers trained on ISTQB will notice. Confirmation testing (does the intended change work) and regression testing (did anything else break) are mixed in one list. A test case is not a test condition, and Teslon counts the same condition several times. Risk level is likelihood × impact, while Teslon's "first" is reach and type, not risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Confirmation testing**: "A type of change-related testing performed after fixing a defect to confirm that a failure caused by that defect does not reoccur" (ISTQB glossary wording, via imbus glossary) — [imbus: confirmation testing](https://www.imbus.de/en/glossar/term/confirmation-testing) [search summary]
- **Regression testing**: "testing of a previously tested component or system after modification to ensure that no new defects have been introduced". The ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-1 variant adds "the sufficiency of a set of regression test cases depends on the item under test and on the modifications to that item or its operational environment" — [imbus: regression testing](https://www.imbus.de/en/glossar/term/regression-testing) [search summary]
- **Impact analysis**: "the identification of all work products affected by a change, including an estimate of the resources needed to accomplish the change" (older ISTQB wording). A newer paraphrase: "identifying the areas of the system that may be affected by a proposed change" to "determine the scope of regression testing" — [ISTQB glossary help PDF](https://api.glossary.istqb.org/storage/help/R0uz58NqLzUz48LVUuyGSF76NFj4LHQazSs0GlNS.pdf); [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/istqb-foundation/testing-throughout-sdlc/confirmation-regression-maintenance) [search summary]. Note that the ISTQB definition includes a **resource estimate**, and Teslon gives effort only if configured (G17).
- **Risk level** (CTFL v4.0, ch. 5.2): determined by likelihood and impact. Quantitative (multiplicative) or qualitative (risk matrix) approaches are both allowed — [ISTQB Ch.5 notes](https://istqbfoundation.pages.dev/ISTQB_Chapter5_Notes.html); [softwaretestpilot](https://softwaretestpilot.com/istqb/glossary/chapter/managing-test-activities) [search summary; study aids, not the official PDF]
- **Exit criteria** (CTFL v4.0): typical measures are coverage level, unresolved defects, defect density and failed tests. Running out of time can be acceptable "if stakeholders accept the residual risk" — [ISTQB Ch.5 notes](https://istqbfoundation.pages.dev/ISTQB_Chapter5_Notes.html) [search summary]
- **Test condition / test basis** definitions were not retrieved from an official source (gap). Standard ISTQB usage, from the 2018/2023 glossary and known wording, so mark as inference: the test basis is the body of knowledge used for test analysis and design; a test condition is a testable aspect of a component or system identified as a basis for testing; a test case adds preconditions, inputs, expected results and postconditions.
- Industry-standard metric definitions: DRE = pre-release defects / (pre-release + production defects), e.g., 90 found in test + 10 after release = 90%. Escaped defects = "defects reported by users of the product after its release". Caveat: DRE "does not differentiate between the severities of defects" — [software.com DRE](https://www.software.com/engineering-metrics/defect-removal-efficiency); [software.com escaped defects](https://www.software.com/engineering-metrics/escaped-defects) [search summary; vendor glossary]

### Inferences (vocabulary map for Teslon)
| Teslon word today | ISTQB-aligned word a lead expects | Suggested use |
|---|---|---|
| "Test" areas with cases | Split into **confirmation tests** (the PR's intended behaviour: future birth date now rejected) and **regression tests** (areas the change reaches that should behave as before) | Two sub-lists. Leads budget them differently. |
| "case" (20) | **test condition** (about 6 here) × **entry point** (UI, API, chat) | Count conditions in the headline; cases are variants. |
| "first" / tier | **risk level** (likelihood × impact) with a stated reason | Show H/M/L with the reason ("user-visible error on a core form"). |
| "Safe to skip" | **out of scope (with rationale)** / "not affected, verified" | Keep "Skip" but add "out of regression scope for this PR"; it reads as a scope decision, not a test result. |
| "Unclear" | **residual risk / not assessed** | "Not assessed: needs a check" fits exit-criteria language (stakeholders accept residual risk). |
| "Data" check, "Watch after release" | **release / deployment checks**, **monitoring** | Release checklist, separate from test scope. |
| "How sure" / confidence | **evidence completeness** and **known limitations** | Fit to an audit trail (ISO 29119-3 test completion report has "deviations" and "residual risks"; inference, standard not read). |

### Gaps
- Official CTFL v4.0, CTAL-TM v3.0, CTAL-TAE v2.0, TMMi and ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119 texts were not read (istqb.org not fetched; budget and host limits). CTAL-TAE v2.0 content on reporting and CI/CD integration, and CTAL-TM v3.0 on risk-based test management and metrics, should be checked by the writer before quoting syllabus section numbers.

## 5. Per-persona specification: what an impact report must give

### Takeaway
Each persona takes a different decision from the same facts. The tester decides what to do in this session and whether what they see is a bug. The SDET decides which automated tests to run or write. The lead decides scope, effort, assignment and the defensible out-of-scope list. The manager decides release risk and whether the process (and the tool) is working. One report can serve all four only if it leads with the shared verdict and then gives each persona its own first view, its own unit (case, test, hour, trend) and its own export.

### Cited Findings (consumption, channels and design practice)
- **PR-comment noise is the main complaint about bots.** Wessel et al. ("Don't Disturb Me") found developers perceive bot behaviour as noise, "a recurrent and central problem", citing "verbosity; high frequency and timing of actions; and unsolicited actions". Code-review bots raised merged PRs but "decreases communication among developers" (study of 1,194 projects) — [NSF PAR: Bots in pull requests](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10356948-bots-pull-requests-good-bad-promising); [USP PDF](https://www.ime.usp.br/~gerosa/papers/ICSMENIER2022.pdf); [arXiv 2103.13950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13950?context=cs) [search summary, 2021-2022]. Teslon already updates one marked comment in place (`COMMENT_MARKER = '<!-- teslon:impact -->'`, `markdown.ts:39`) and caps its size (`markdown.ts:154`). Both are good practice by this evidence.
- **Overview first, details on demand.** Dashboard guidance follows Shneiderman's mantra ("overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand"): 4-6 top KPIs, then click into charts, then records. One layered view can serve executives, managers and analysts — [BI book 15.4](https://ljkelly3141.github.io/bi-book/15.4-dashboard-design-principles.html); [xylitytech](https://xylitytech.com/data-analytics/data-visualization-strategy-information-design/) [search summary; a vendor claim of 20-40% task-completion gains is untraceable and excluded]
- **Teamscale test-gap treemap (praised model for managers and leads):** methods coloured "unchanged (gray), changed & tested (green), changed & untested (yellow), and new & untested (red)". Clicking opens change and test history. Teamscale says the aim is to prevent changes "slipping through testing without you knowing", not to close all gaps, and warns "an all-green Test Gap treemap does not give you any guarantees". Vendor claims: untested changes are "5x" more likely to contain a defect, and at Munich Re "over 50% of the changes were not tested" across two releases — [Teamscale docs: test gap treemaps](https://docs.teamscale.com/howto/working-with-test-gap-treemaps/); [Teamscale TGA reference](https://docs.teamscale.com/reference/test-gap-analysis); [2016 paper](https://teamscale.com/hubfs/26978363/Publications/2016-did-we-test-the-right-thing-experiences-with-test-gap-analysis-in-practice.pdf?hsLang=en) [search summary; vendor]
- **Allure / ReportPortal criticism** (mostly from a competitor, TestDino, so one-sided): stakeholders, "especially project managers, struggle to interpret reports". Allure is "a static artifact rather than a live dashboard" and lacks persistent history. ReportPortal "may feel dated for stakeholders who expect polished, role-specific views" and needs Docker Compose and a database to run. Defenders praise ReportPortal's failure clustering and custom dashboards — [TestDino: outgrowing Allure](https://testdino.com/blog/outgrows-allure-reports); [dev.to ReportPortal alternatives](https://dev.to/pratik01/finding-smarter-reportportal-alternatives-2kpc) [search summary; vendor-biased]. Release checklist (data, monitoring, deploy, external callers). | Per area: all cases and all proofs. Changed-file ledger. "Not followed by this build" limitations. |
| **Test manager: "Release"** | Build/release roll-up across PRs. Risk summary in business terms. Trend tiles (escapes in skipped areas, DRE, cycle time, hours saved), each with counts beside percentages. Audit record (who accepted skips). Tool accuracy on this repo. | Per PR verdicts. Export (PDF sign-off, CSV/JSON for BI). |

### Layer 2 rules (apply everywhere)
- Progressive disclosure: verdict, then role view, then area, then case, then evidence hop. Never more than one click from a number to its proof.
- Plain words first, code identifiers second (G14). Never strike-through for "not affected" (G15).
- Every uncertainty is shown at the level where it changes a decision: an unclear area in the scope table, an unseen consumer in the risk line, a hedged outcome as "Unknown: observe", never "Expect" (G4, G5).
- Colour plus icon plus word, and icons that do not collide with test-result semantics (✅ = passed) (G9; WCAG 1.4.1).
- Same numbers in every channel (Teslon's single `brief()` already guarantees this; `areas.ts:3-8`).
- One comment per PR, updated in place, capped (already done; Wessel et al. on bot noise).
